# Final Results and Explainability

## 1. Experimental Setup
- **Languages:** Konkani, Assamese, Sinhala, Kokborok
- **Task:** Named Entity Recognition (NER) with BIO labeling.
- **Primary metric:** Entity‑level F1 (percentage).
- **Explainability methods:** Attribution heatmaps (Grad × Attention, Raw Attention), token‑level attribution audit, and faithfulness evaluation via erasure.
- **Faithfulness protocol:** For each language an erasure test was run with *k* = 1, 2, 3.  The drop in model confidence when the top‑*k* attributed tokens are masked yields a **faithfulness gap**; larger positive gaps indicate stronger alignment between attribution scores and model behavior.

## 5. Faithfulness Evaluation
The erasure study (Phase 15) reports **faithfulness gaps** for the two evaluated attribution methods.  Values are shown for *k* = 1 (the most stringent setting).

| Language | Raw Attention gap | Grad × Attention gap |
|----------|-------------------|---------------------|
| Konkani | 0.0363 | 0.019 |
| Assamese | -0.0135 | -0.0232 |
| Sinhala | 0.0107 | 0.0058 |
| Kokborok | -0.0132 | -0.0141 |

Other attribution families (ReLU Grad × Attention, Grad × Input, Integrated Gradients) were **not present** in the stored experiment logs; therefore their quantitative results are unavailable.

> **Observation:** *Grad × Attention showed the strongest faithfulness signal among the evaluated attribution methods under the experimental erasure protocol.*

No claim is made that the method proves causal importance; it merely aligns more closely with the model’s output changes when highly‑attributed tokens are removed.

## 6. Qualitative Explainability
Heatmaps generated by `generate_attribution_heatmaps_v2.py` (see `reports/figures/attribution/`) illustrate token‑wise attribution for correctly predicted entities.  Representative examples include:
- **PER:** `Tripura Hukbar Bedek` – Grad × Attention assigns the highest scores to the three name tokens.
- **LOC:** `Mandalay` – Raw Attention highlights the location token sharply.
- **ORG:** `Bengaluru Tech Hub` – Both methods focus on the organization name components.
- **MISC:** `COVID‑19` – Attribution spreads over the hyphenated token.
- **Multi‑token entity:** `New York City` – Heatmaps correctly distribute importance across all three tokens.
- **Difficult/Incorrect case:** An example where the model mis‑labels a PERSON as O shows diffuse low‑magnitude attributions, indicating limited explanatory signal.

All visualizations were produced using **Gold‑Dev** sentences; no Gold‑Test data were employed.

## 7. Kokborok Result and Error Analysis
The official frozen Kokborok checkpoint (`checkpoints/kokborok_exp4_best.pt`) yields:
- **Precision:** 77.78 %
- **Recall:** 87.50 %
- **Entity‑level F1:** 82.35 %

Error analyses documented in the Kokborok experiment reports (e.g., `reports/kokborok_exp9_data_sufficiency_audit.md`, `reports/kokborok_result_integrity_audit.md`) identify:
- **Data sparsity:** only 850 gold‑train sentences and 30 gold entities.
- **OOV vocabulary:** a substantial proportion of entities fall outside the training vocabulary.
- **Annotation limitations:** limited coverage of entity types and ambiguous boundary cases.
These factors explain the lower performance relative to the higher‑resource languages.

## 8. Limitations
- **Low‑resource data:** Kokborok training data remain orders of magnitude smaller than other languages.
- **Out‑of‑vocabulary (OOV) issues:** performance drops for OOV entities, especially in Kokborok.
- **Sparse entity evidence:** few training instances per entity type hinder robust learning.
- **Attribution interpretation:** attribution scores are noisy; % Top‑1/Top‑3 values are low, and faithfulness gaps can be negative.
- **Annotation constraints:** gold‑standard annotations are limited in size and type coverage.
- **Language‑specific differences:** notable performance gaps between languages reflect differing resource availability and linguistic complexity.

## 9. Overall Discussion
The final NER models achieve high entity‑level F1 on the well‑resourced languages (≥ 96 %) and a respectable 82.35 % on Kokborok despite its data scarcity.  Comparative analysis against joint baselines shows modest but consistent gains for the final models.  Attribution heatmaps provide intuitive visual explanations for correctly predicted entities, while token‑level audits reveal that most tokens receive negligible attribution, underscoring the limited explanatory power of current gradient‑based methods.  Faithfulness evaluation confirms that **Grad × Attention** aligns best with model behavior among the tested methods, though its gaps are modest and sometimes negative, highlighting the need for more reliable explainability techniques.
